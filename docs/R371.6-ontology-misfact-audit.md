# R371.6 — Ontology / knowledge-graph mis-fact audit (adversarial)

**Date:** 2026-08-18 · **Scope:** the R371 role × risk-class obligation layer — wiring,
the live knowledge graph, and the ontology matrix it projects — audited adversarially
against the pinned statute corpus and the live wire. **Result: 16 direction-inverted
matrix bindings found, verified, and removed (90 → 74); the R371 flag stays OFF
(rejected R371.5) and its block now derives from a statute-clean matrix.**

---

## 1. What was audited and how

The audit followed the actual pipeline the model sees, not the data tables in isolation:

1. **Code wiring** — `REGENOLD_ROLE_OBLIGATION_CONTEXT` → `role_obligation_context_enabled()`
   (fresh read, gated by master `REGENOLD_KG_CONTEXT`) → `fetch_role_risk_class_context()`
   (`_ROLE_RISK_CLASS_CYPHER`, matches only `$ids` already cited → non-citable, hard
   rule #10) → render as `KNOWLEDGE-GRAPH ROLE x RISK-CLASS DUTIES`, budget-reserved in
   `_R326_RESERVED_MARKERS`, registered in `_engine_cache_key` (R350 trap closed).
   **Wiring is correct.**
2. **Backend reality** — the kg_context fetchers read whatever graph backend is active:
   `REGENOLD_GRAPH_BACKEND=embedded` disables Aura entirely (`execute_read` → `[]`),
   so **all graph context blocks render EMPTY under the golden-run env**. The production
   default is `neo4j` (Aura, active here via `.env`). Measured both:
   - embedded: `fetch_deontic_context` = 0 rows, `render_kg_context` = 0 blocks.
   - Aura: deontic block renders and is **clean** (see §2).
3. **Statute verification** — every suspect binding checked against the SHA-pinned corpus
   (`app.data.official_eu_ai_act.OFFICIAL_ARTICLE_TEXT`).
4. **Wire-level intercepts** — `_build_role_obligation_answer` (the deterministic
   role-obligation path, live since R130) renders the matrix as "X are bound by …" and
   seeds it into `context.obligations` → the Stage-2 citable universe. Measured the
   exact wire strings before/after.
5. **Gold-refutation double-check** — the one removal a gold pair could refute
   (Art. 13 on deployers) was tested **live** (Bedrock, 2 rows) and judged (§5).
6. **Leakage probe** — all 140 branch answers from the R371.5 powered gate scanned for
   wrong-direction claims about the audited refs (§6).
7. **Veto-row analysis** — the 6 rows that drove the R371.5 veto examined for factual
   corruption vs citation contraction (§7).

| 6–9 | `DOWNSTREAM_PROVIDER × {gpai, gpai_systemic} → Art. 53 / Art. 55 / Art. 89 / Annex XII` | Art. 53(1)(b) + Annex XII are the **GPAI model provider's** supply duties; Art. 55 binds the systemic-risk **model provider**; Art. 89 is "**Monitoring actions**" by the **AI Office** (downstream providers hold a complaint *right*, Art. 89(2)). The explanation *duty* sits on the deployer (Art. 86(1)) — already bound under `DEPLOYER × high_risk_annex_iii` and retained. | "Affected persons of a high-risk AI system listed in Annex III are bound by Art. 85, and Art. 86" |

Also fixed in the prose file (`app/data/role_obligations.py`): Art. 56 removed from
`gpai_provider` / `gpai_systemic_provider` `primary_articles` (same statute basis). The
live graph carries no Art. 56 `HAS_OBLIGATION_ARTICLE` edge, so the deontic block is
unchanged; `articles_for_role()` (semantic-validator anchors) now excludes the wrong duty.

**Documented, NOT changed:** Art. 4 (AI literacy) is bound only at `MINIMAL_RISK` in the
matrix. Art. 4(1) actually binds providers *and* deployers of **all** AI systems; the
matrix entry is true-but-non-exhaustive (under-scope, not a false claim), and widening it
to every risk class would inflate the deterministic wire. Recorded as a known limitation.
